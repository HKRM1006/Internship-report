# Summary Report: "FCAJ - Agentic AI Build Week"

### Event Objectives

- Create a space for teams that participated in the **Agentic AI Build Week (AABW)** to return and share their real-world journeys: from ideation and product building to demoing and receiving results.
- Spread practical experience when working with Agentic AI on AWS rather than stopping at theory.
- Inspire and equip a practical perspective for those who are about to or are currently self-studying and building personal workshops on Agentic AI.
- Point out mistakes, difficulties, and ways to overcome time pressure when building AI products in a short timeframe.
- Suggest ways to apply the processes, design mindset, and experiences of the teams to self-practice and build personal projects.

### List of Sharing Teams

- **OneTeam** (Anh Duy, Tran Dong, Doan Trung, Minh Viet, Anshul Roy) - shared their journey of building the **KFC Bot Agent**.
- **3KA** (Huynh An Khuong, Nguyen Quoc Huy, Ngo Quang Khoi, Hoang Le Thanh Duc, Dang Nguyen Phuoc Loc, Dang Truong Hung) - shared their 24-hour journey of building **S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D**.
- **Plan V** (Pham Tien Thuan Phat, Huynh Hoang Long, Le Minh Nghia, Tran Dai Vi, Nguyen An) - shared the process of building the **Solution Architect Professional Native App**.
- **Signal Scout Team** (Le Tan Luc, Do Hoang Hieu, Trieu Quoc Hao, Nguyen Van Duy Khiem, Nguyen Cong Minh, Nguyen Tran Minh Quan) - shared their journey of building **Signal Scout**.

### Key Highlights

#### The emotional journey of a hackathon (3KA)

- Before the competition day, the whole team shared the common mindset of first-timers: fear of not being good enough, fear of failure, not knowing where to start, and fear of running out of time.
- Over 24 hours, emotions went through 3 distinct stages: **Doubt** (confusion, not knowing where to start) - **Flow** (focus, ideas starting to take shape) - **Pride** (proud of actually building a product).
- The biggest challenge was not a lack of AI knowledge or using AWS for the first time, but maintaining alertness and teamwork spirit while sleep deprived and dealing with continuous code errors.
- Takeaway: **the final result is not as important as what is learned along the way** - this is also the core spirit that the sharing session aimed to convey.

#### From real-world problems to products - how teams think about problems (OneTeam, Signal Scout)

- OneTeam started from a true story (McDonald's stopping its AI drive-thru test) to point out that: the problem is not whether "AI can do it," but **correctly understanding the core system problem** behind it (messy data, strict business rules, mandatory verification before execution).
- How the team handled it: designed the Agent following a clear loop **Goal → Plan → Tools → Act → Verify**, and structured the architecture as "adding rather than rebuilding" (adding a channel = adding an adapter, adding business logic = adding a connector) - a mindset that can be applied to any personal project looking to scale later.
- Signal Scout shared how they used the **Value Creation & Delivery Canvas** to clarify the business problem before writing code, and specifically the habit of **estimating operating costs across multiple scenarios (Min/Mid/Max)** right from the ideation stage - something many self-taught learners often overlook.

#### Working under pressure of time and limited resources (3KA, Plan V)

- 3KA shared honestly about technical difficulties encountered: unstable live video, high inference latency, losing object tracking across frames, never having used AWS before - but still completing the product by breaking down problems and prioritizing the core first.
- Plan V shared their experience transitioning from a manual process (reading requirement docs manually, drawing architecture from scratch, guessing costs based on experience) to an AI-assisted step-by-step process - demonstrating the value of **automating repetitive parts to reserve time for parts requiring deep thinking**.

### Key Takeaways

#### Start from the problem, not the technology

All teams spent the initial time thoroughly understanding the real-world problem before choosing which AWS tools to use. When doing a personal workshop, clearly write down "what problem I am solving" before opening the IDE.

#### Design Agents using the Goal - Plan - Tools - Act - Verify loop

This is the easiest thinking framework to adopt when building a truly "actionable" Agent rather than just a conversational one - it can serve as a structural backbone for any personal project regarding Agentic AI.

#### Estimate costs and design for scalability from the start

Even for a personal project or small workshop, cultivate the habit of outlining preliminary operating costs and designing modular/adapter-based architectures instead of writing code that only runs once for a demo.

#### Embrace failure and learn from speed, not perfection

The biggest lesson from 3KA's sharing: just start building even without a solid foundation, fix errors as you encounter them - this mindset directly applies to self-practice and solo workshop creation without waiting until you are "good enough to start."

### Event Experience

- The atmosphere of the sharing session was close and authentic: teams not only showcased great products but also recounted "behind-the-scenes" moments - code bugs, sleep deprivation, confusion - helping listeners (especially beginners) reduce pressure and find motivation to start building rather than just learning theory.
- Gained a comprehensive view of an Agentic AI project from an insider's perspective: from ideation, architecture, and costs, to genuine emotions during development - things that reading documentation or watching pure demos cannot convey.
